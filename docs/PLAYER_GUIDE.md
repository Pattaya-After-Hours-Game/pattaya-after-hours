# Running your first bar

Start the game from the repository with `godot --path .`, or run a packaged
build. On the startup menu, choose **New Game** to open your first bar or
**Resume** to continue your saved campaign. Starting another new game asks you
to confirm erasing the previous campaign, including its backup; **Cancel** keeps
it. Resume is disabled when no readable save is available.

The selected bar’s name appears at the top left. Open **Tutorial** for a short practice shift.
Buy a pack, serve an order, help an impatient guest, hire a bartender and close
for the night. Practice uses its own till and does not change your save.
Tap a top-bar stat on mobile or hover over it on desktop for a quick explanation.
Double-click the rainbow duo in the bar, or tap them on a touchscreen, to see
them kiss with a floating heart.

## Drink prices and restocking money

House beers are ฿80 in beer bars, ฿100 in hostess bars and ฿120 in gogos.
Service shows the current standard quote and the cost of your next delivery.
Happy Hour discounts new orders by 20%; already-waiting orders keep their quote.
The premium-menu upgrade unlocks mango cocktails at ฿30 above house lager; renovation alone adds none.

House lager stock begins at ฿38 each: ฿228 for six. Supplier upgrades switch to
12 for ฿432, then ฿408/384/360. Set aside the whole delivery bill before spending
on improvements. A new game starts with twelve drinks and ฿228; updating an
existing campaign does not change its cash, stock, waiting tabs or paid stock costs.

## Hire Gabby

In **Service → Team**, choose **Hire Gabby · ฿1,200** when a floor
position is free. She has the standard ten-day bartender contract, wages and
25-second service, improved through normal training. Each real drink she serves
earns an extra ฿5 in the nightly tip jar. Owner service and offline estimates
do not earn her bonus. Existing matched-drink bonuses still apply. Gabby keeps
her identity and training through expiry and renewal; renew her at her assigned
venue. There is one Gabby across the street, and she works as a bartender.

## Hire Daisy

At a **Hostess bar**, open **Service → Team → Hire Daisy · ฿4,500**.
Daisy is a separate adult entertainer with a ten-day host contract, normal host
wages and one floor position. Keep the next shift's payroll after hiring.
She attracts visitors and uses the existing host training and booking controls;
she does not pour drinks or earn Gabby's service bonus. Her rota, expiry and
bookings control whether she appears in the room. Only one Daisy can be hired
across the street. Renew her existing slot with **Rehire Daisy · ฿4,500**;
her training and any pending return time remain intact.

## Find the next useful step

**Service** opens on a short overview with one goal and its progress. Use
**Fridge** for drinks, **Team** for staff, **Upgrades** for equipment and
automation, **Reports** for finances and tips, and **Hours** for cleaning,
closing and guided shifts. Tap **?** beside a goal or hiring choice for a
slide-out explanation. **Back** stays in the title bar as you scroll. The highlighted goal button takes you to the
matching control or tab. It does not buy anything. Early steps cover real drink
sales and regulars; later ones explain renovation, reopening preparations,
deliveries and the first manager. The guide follows the Blue Hour and then the
street-wide story even when you have another property selected.

Final renovations now cost ฿40,000 for a beer bar, ฿65,000 for a Hostess bar and
฿120,000 for a gogo. Each still needs its own 450 happy visits, 70 regulars and
40 healthy shifts, plus operating cash. You can work on another bar while that
customer base grows. The first renovation and ordinary drink prices are unchanged.

## Try a faster shift

In **Journal**, choose **Start tuning playtest · 2×**. The street runs at double
speed in a disposable copy of your campaign. Toggle back to 1×, adjust passing
crowds or owner cooldowns, or trigger Rat Plague during quiet trading. Return to
**Journal → End playtest** to pick up your original campaign where you left it.

## Read The Soi Dispatch

New street events, celebrations and late-night raids open a short newspaper
notice. Open **Events** from the paper to act, or **Back to the bar** to dismiss
it. When a story draws on real reporting, the paper includes a short note and
a button that opens the original article in your browser. Cart Jam and Bo’s
Bed Race take over the game screen until you finish or leave.

- **Encore Rush** offers a solo rush or paid relief during the warning.
- **Cart Jam** stops deliveries at the affected bar. Book a hand delivery in
  advance, wait for the jam to end, or play the puzzle. Drag vehicles along
  their lanes to free Bo’s van at the right-hand exit. On desktop, arrow keys
  also move the selected vehicle.
- **Last Call of the Dead** offers a costume contest that fills seats with
  guests who can order up to three rounds.
- **Bo’s Bed Race** takes one bartender off the floor. Tap **Push** or press
  Space to reach the finish line before time runs out and earn a trophy.
- **Lucky Lizard** lets you clear a path for rescuers and can bring in locals
  for a lucky round afterward.
- **Definitely Not Godzilla** offers a photo stop for the robot dinosaur with
  a short slowdown while you set up, followed by a visitor rush.

## See when tours bring in customers

Open **Tours**, choose a guide, then assign a route. The guide recommends your
street for the current trading shift. Use **Back** to return to the guide list.
When a referral gets a seat, the top-left update names the guide and venue.
The badge above **Tours** counts active agreements and shows a red **!** when
there are none. The guide list shows each agreement's remaining trading time
or its **Expired** status.

## When the rats come out

A rare **Rat Plague** can hit during live trading. The swarm drives seated
customers out and scares away most walk-ins for 45 seconds. During the
20-second warning, **Call Bo’s rat patrol** in Events for ฿120 to keep seated
guests inside and save some passing trade.

## Look ahead to day seven

Open **Events** to see the next free neighbourhood celebration. **Anong's
Birthday** starts at noon on day seven and ends at the normal 1am close the
following calendar day. A banner appears two game days ahead. No hire, manager,
entry fee or paid preparation is required.

Clean the Blue Hour, fill its fridge, check contracts and prepare to serve. The
party adds 40% shared street traffic and more appeal for the host bar. Visitors
can request up to three drinks if affordable, with repeat timing of 26 seconds
or 19 with music (14/11/8 with later sound tiers). They plan one extra round,
capped at three, and wait ten seconds longer.
Full venues still turn people away, and slow or empty bars still lose orders.
Drinks and pool retain their prices; free-pool promotions remain separate.

The actual party report in Events and Journal shows receipts, consumed-stock
costs, wages/overhead, commission, contribution and new regulars. It excludes
manager income and unused stock purchases and does not pay another bonus.
Next come the Regulars' Reunion, Pim's Mixtape Party and Bo's Thank-You Night,
on days fourteen, twenty-one and twenty-eight, then rotating weekly.

Offline time does not advance the live date, consume the party or earn its
bonus. Later saves get the next scheduled event rather than retroactive cash.

**Reputation attracts visitors; it does not speed their drinking.** More visitors
can mean more total orders, but stock, seats and service still limit sales. Music
and parties shorten repeat timing. Fridges earn reputation through happy visits.
A signed number floats beside **Reputation** whenever it changes. Gains appear
in teal and losses appear in red.

## Is my bartender working?

Look at **BARTENDERS** in the top right. Each row shows a portrait, level and
red cooldown bar that fills until ready. Level 1 means no training; each training
purchase adds one level. The label explains no orders, no stock, off duty,
finished contracts or venue closure. Scroll for larger teams. Tap a row or the
arrow for Service and detailed actual-sale counts.

The **Tip jar** beneath the list is this venue's shared unpaid total, including
first-visit tips, matched-drink bonuses and Gabby's bonus. It pays at closing;
it is not an individual bartender's lifetime earnings.

She starts ready and automatically serves a waiting customer on the next live
tick if stock is available. Then she recovers for 25 seconds, improving to 20/15
with basic training, then 12/10 at stage two for ฿1,200/1,600. Your clicks and a temporarily empty queue cannot restart her wait.
An empty bar can leave her ready, but it never banks multiple drinks. Her serving
pose now follows an actual sale. Ready staff get the first order before owner
auto-serve; both still respect the same real stock and customers.

**Owner auto-serve now costs ฿3,000.** It automates your Serve button at its current
speed as relief: staff lead quiet service; it helps when orders outnumber on-duty
bartenders or a guest would leave before staff recover (ten seconds or less left).
With no available bartender it covers all orders. Manual taps still work normally.
Existing purchases remain owned; there
is no refund or new purchase required.

## Empty fridge and not enough cash?

Look directly below **Buy drinks** for **Bo's recovery delivery · 6 drinks**.
It appears when you have no stock and cannot afford your current pack. You can
use it while open: waiting orders stay, staff go off duty, and you take over
serving. Anong covers overhead for two minutes. Bo takes ฿14 per consignment
drink when sold. Repeated clicks cannot add more stock or restart that cover.

At ฿39, three ordinary ฿80 beers leave ฿237 after repaying Bo: enough for a normal
฿228 six-pack. Nida no longer charges commission on these or other drinks. Budget room and cash for the whole pack as usual. Closed venues also
retain Owner-run recovery; raids/closed hours must finish before reopening.

**Nightly tips:** one served visit adds ฿10 pending, regardless of how many rounds
that guest buys. All bars' jars are counted once after the last nightly closure,
before clean-up. Service and Journal show the amounts. No customer served means
no tip. Pool and manager income do not tip; offline estimates never add tips.
The pending jar is not spendable until counted. Keep stock money aside meanwhile.

## Your first shift

1. Serve the three opening customers. A yellow/gold dot marks an order; red means
   ten seconds or less before the guest loses patience or their visit ends. Each successful
   click sells one drink and consumes one unit of stock, then refills for ten seconds.
   No order or unavailable stock means no spent cooldown.
2. When no orders are waiting, welcome passersby. Welcoming has a cooldown and a
   capped effect; it cannot create an unlimited crowd.
3. Open **Service**. Read demand, staff service, occupied seats and stock together.
   Manual serving helps when staff are absent or cannot keep up. “Forecast automatic net” is
   not a prediction that you will earn nothing while actively serving.
4. Restock before the fridge empties. **Buy 6 drinks** starts at ฿228 and requires room for all
   six and the full price. Supplier level 2 changes this to **Buy 12 drinks for ฿432**:
   cheaper per drink, but you need twelve free spaces and the entire bill. Failed purchases change nothing. Stock costs money when delivered, while
   wages and overhead accrue during open trading.
5. Buy the improvement that addresses your current limit. Keep money for the
   next shift rather than spending the till down to zero.

Wall fans appear after buying **Fans & comfortable seats**. They remain installed
when the venue closes, but stop spinning. Renovating does not buy this equipment.
Buying **Sports screen** puts a TV on the wall and unlocks Sports Night. The room
and property thumbnail reflect the purchase immediately, including on saved games.
Fitted screens in beer-bar stages 2 and 3 stay dark until you buy the upgrade.

The first useful purchase depends on the bar: frontage helps quiet trade,
seating helps a full room, and a bartender helps unattended orders. More service
capacity cannot sell drinks to customers who are not there.

## Pick what to stock

A dimmed purchase shows what you still need in the till, such as **฿178 short**.
That figure includes any operating reserve the purchase requires.

Tap **Stock** to open the drink list. If a stocked fridge can’t fill any
waiting orders, Serve names the missing drink, for example **Need Rum & cola**.
The gold **Needed now** section groups drinks whose orders exceed available stock.
Each drink has its own icon and the Buy button shows the full pack price.
When space runs out, tap **Return** on spare drinks to send back up to three. Tap
a drink’s **?** for expected popularity, margin, recent orders and crowd trends.

Locals lean toward house lager and budget lime soda. Visitors order more Rum &
cola. Big spenders are likelier to order Mango cocktails once you unlock the
stage-2 premium menu. Happy Hour can put pricier drinks within a guest’s budget.
Serve a guest’s requested specialty drink on their first round for a chance at a **฿5 tip**.

Buy the **Supplier** upgrade to unlock **Auto-restock**. Bo buys packs of the
drinks you already carry when orders come in and stock runs low. Returned stock
pays back half its purchase cost.

Bo occasionally offers **12 discounted specialty drinks** in the afternoon.
The popup shows your wholesale saving, the till after buying, and how far that
would leave you from your current cash goal. Tap **Not now** to pass. You can
reopen the deal from **Fridge** until 3:15pm.

## Give yourself a faster shift

Open **Service → Upgrades** or tap the number badge on Service. Current-stage
upgrades within budget come first, followed by those that need more cash.
Below the divider, the renovation shows what the next stage requires.
Its upgrades are previewed in the same budget order, greyed out until
the venue reaches that stage and meets each purchase’s other requirements.
Choose **Ready only** to filter purchases, or **Show all** to restore the list.

Tap **Unlock stage** near the top to jump to the selected venue’s renovation.
Stage 2 expands the venue and opens new upgrades. It needs **70 happy visits,
10 regulars and 8 healthy shifts** there, plus the renovation price and an operating
reserve. Stage 3 needs **450 happy visits, 70 regulars and 40 healthy shifts**.
The panel shows live progress and the cash shortfall.

**What counts?** explains the local gates. A healthy report needs at least four
sales, 80% of orders fulfilled and positive customer profit after consumed stock
and operating costs. Reports settle every two minutes of street trading and at
closing. Manager income, nightly tips and another bar’s customers cannot qualify
this venue. Use **Reports** to find a supply, service or demand problem; quiet
venues can put surplus staff off duty.

The action buttons fill back up as foreground time passes. Welcome is green
when there’s a free seat, Chat is green when a guest needs reassurance, and
Serve is green when an order is waiting. Each refill greys out when its
condition ends. The labels show readiness or what’s blocking the action.
Bartenders keep serving while your own Serve button refills.

Near the top of **Service**, **Faster pouring** and **Better welcoming** each have
three purchases: **10 → 8 → 6 → 4 seconds**, costing **฿900 / ฿1,400 / ฿2,800**.
The menu shows cash still needed, including the next shift’s operating reserve.
Buying a level changes the next use; it does not clear an active refill. Individual
staff training improves bartenders separately and survives rehiring.

**Have a chat** helps the eligible guest nearest walking out, when they have ten
seconds or less left to wait. It adds ten seconds of patience and visit time,
once per visit. You still need to fulfil their orders to earn satisfaction.
The order dot returns to yellow if the chat gives them more than ten seconds left.
Chat and Welcome share a timer: welcoming uses its current cooldown; chatting
locks both for twenty seconds, even with upgraded welcoming. Serve stays separate.
This creates a choice between attracting newcomers and looking after a queue.

An active opening can buy pouring speed, frontage, seating and pool before hiring,
while restocking by hand. Delaying automatic deliveries and a larger fridge until
stage 2 leaves money for the renovation. A signage-and-staff opening also works;
neither manual upgrades nor Chat is required to finish. Avoid buying every speed
level when stock, seats or customers are your actual limit.

Closing a venue does not reset timers. Normal prep time refills them; skipping to
noon, paused decisions, startup menus and offline time do not. Reloading preserves
the remaining refill and which visitors have already had a chat.

A short two-note bell means the selected venue has an order waiting. It repeats
at most every ten seconds while orders remain; quickly emptying and refilling the
queue cannot spam it. It is silent in practice, fights and street exploration,
and follows **Sound / Muted**. A falling order count can mean a served drink or
an impatient customer leaving; use drink sales and the shift's lost-service or
lost-stock counts to distinguish them.

## The clock and tomorrow's opening

The clock advances one game minute per real second. **DAY** above it shows your
current in-game campaign day, starting at **Day 1** and continuing across saves.
Offline estimates and skipping from closing to noon do not add extra days.
Every venue type opens at
12pm and normally shuts at 1am. Dates change at midnight; two-minute business
reports and the 30-second pool timer still use real seconds. Financial rates
are shown per real minute.

Check the peak indicator in Service when planning staffing and stock:

| Venue | Peak hours |
| --- | --- |
| Beer bar | 5–9pm |
| Hostess bar | 7–11pm |
| Gogo | 10pm–2am |

Peaks add 50% attraction, increasing your share of the existing street traffic.
They do not guarantee sales or extra service capacity. Each drink sold during a
peak adds two dirt points instead of one, so a busy night needs more cleanup.
Gogos still need permission to operate during their peak hour after 1am.

Before 1am, use **Stay open until 3am tonight** in Service for any venue you want
to keep trading. A warning appears at 12:30am. You can cancel the extension or
close early. No venue trades after 3am, and the choice resets at noon.

Each late venue adds one heat point per ten minutes of actual late operation.
Every fifteen late minutes it risks a raid: 2% plus 0.2 percentage points per
heat point, up to 22%. The fine is ฿300 per venue stage, capped at the cash you
have, and that venue shuts until noon. Fines appear in its report and the weekly
books. Heat cools by one point per two safe real minutes; skipping does not cool
it. Reopening buttons cannot override a raid.

When closed, Service shows **Prep for opening**. Start a fifteen-second clean,
buy stock, and check the rota. Nightly clean-up now starts automatically for everyone. Each drink
sold adds dirt; a completely dirty bar loses 25% of its attraction. Cleaning
costs no cash. Normal stock follows the current supplier pack price and fridge capacity.

After 1am, when all venues have stopped trading, tap **Skip to open** on the bottom bar to jump to noon.
The list shows unfinished prep across the street. You can skip anyway: incomplete
cleaning is cancelled, dirt stays, and no stock is purchased. Waiting naturally
until noon also cancels incomplete cleaning. Skipping does not run events,
complete jobs, award sales or grant healthy-shift progress.

Scheduled closure pauses sales and operating costs. Noon makes venues available
again, but preserves owner closures and cash-shortage shutdowns. Use **Enable
opening** when ready, or the existing owner-run recovery option after a raid
has ended. Street-event countdowns pause while all venues are shut; temporary
closure and cleaning timers still run during foreground prep time.

Customers appear inside the doorway, in front of the furniture and service
counters; bartenders work behind the counters. Smaller passersby stay in the
street opening and do not count toward occupancy. Pool players stay beside
their assigned table, with its paid/free timer on the table front.

## Read the bottleneck

The top bar shows occupied and empty seats. One seat left turns amber. At
capacity it reads **Full**, and **Welcome customers** switches to **Bar full**.
The **Serve** button shows how many orders are waiting.

| What you see | What to try |
| --- | --- |
| Few visitors and staff waiting | Welcome, improve frontage or try an appropriate promotion |
| Orders waiting with stock available | Serve manually, add a bartender or train existing bartenders |
| Full venue and rejected arrivals | Add seating, renovate when eligible, or stop advertising harder |
| Empty stock and lost orders | Buy stock; consider a bigger fridge and automatic deliveries |
| Sales but negative recent net | Check inventory purchases, wages and promotion costs in the shift report |
| Dancers but no automatic sales | Hire or reassign a bartender; dancers attract customers, not serve drinks |
| Plenty of money but a locked renovation | Build that venue's satisfied visits, regulars and healthy shifts |

Recent net records actual cash movements. A large stock delivery can make the
current report look worse even though some inventory will sell next shift.
Forecasts are estimates of automatic operation, not money already in the till.

## Pool and promotions

The first beer-bar pool table costs ฿2,000. Two customers pay **฿50 total** when a
30-second game starts. You do not click the table to collect another fee. Pool
players remain customers and can order drinks. A table uses space that could
otherwise seat visitors, and players stay longer.

Open **Events** for venue promotions:

| Promotion | What changes |
| --- | --- |
| Free Pool Night | New games are free; drinks keep ordinary prices; attracts pool visitors |
| Happy Hour | Newly placed drink orders are discounted by 20%; existing quotes are unchanged |
| Pool Tournament | Pool stays paid; the promotion includes a prize-budget cost |
| Sports Night | Requires a screen and attracts visitors for the fixture |

Only one promotion can run at a venue at once. It lasts two real minutes, followed by
a two-minute cooldown. Repeating the same offer immediately after cooldown has
less pull; vary it or allow another ordinary shift. Paid games stay paid, and
free games stay free even when a promotion ends mid-game.

Free pool works best when you have spare room and can sell the visitors drinks.
It can underperform in a packed bar. Check actual receipts, foregone fees,
attributed drinks and costs in the promotion report. Its baseline visitor figure
is an estimate, not extra customers or income credited to you.

## Staff, management and growth

Bartender contracts cost **฿1,200 for ten game days**, the same upfront as Nida.
Hosts and dancers remain ฿4,500. All floor staff retain wages while on duty;
bartenders do not take Nida’s drink commission.
A base bartender serves one real drink order every **25 seconds** (2.4/min), so
keep serving alongside them during rushes. **Train** on their individual card
costs ฿400 for level 1 (20 seconds) and ฿800 for level 2 (15 seconds). Training
does not create customers and is retained across contracts and role changes.

**Service → Team** shows the remaining contract time. When it expires,
the person stops working and stops drawing wages. Their card stays available;
**Rehire** costs ฿1,200 for bartenders (฿4,500 for hosts/dancers) for another ten days and keeps their training. Rehiring is
manual, never an automatic charge. Existing saves retain their hires and apply
previous venue-wide training to those people; new hires start untrained.

One game day is 24 real minutes before skips. Closed hours, off-duty time and
skipped nights still use the contract. Offline absence also counts at one game
minute per real second, even though the displayed live calendar pauses. At
normal hours with overnight skips, ten trading days are about 130 real minutes.
The manager appointment and supplier agreement remain permanent purchases.

Adult hosts and dancers attract their audience and use the same contracts; they
do not serve drinks. Use each person's rota to pause wages, not their contract.
At Hostess bars and gogos, role reassignment changes service and payroll. Switching
a bartender to a host/dancer charges the contract-price difference for the
remaining term, keeping training and requiring the next shift’s reserve. Switching
back does not refund it.
The **Good Company** card lets hosts earn from guests who want conversation,
music or pool. An interested guest who has bought a first drink can sponsor one
hosted round per visit. **Match** means she shares the guest's favourite topic,
adding **฿40** to the quoted price. Base prices are **฿180/220/260** at host levels
1/2/3, so May and Fah benefit from training without changing their preferences.

Choose **Host round / Match**, then use the normal **Serve** button or let a
bartender pour one stocked house lager. No service means no sale. The order waits
at most thirty seconds; once served, the host spends twenty seconds with the
guest while keeping her appeal. **POUR** and **CHEERS** markers show the activity.
Host and guest cannot take a barfine booking during that round.

Three finished rounds fill the **Room Buzz** meter for thirty seconds of **+2
appeal**. It attracts a larger share of existing street traffic, not guaranteed
new customers. There is no extra cash reward. Interrupted company and unserved
orders do not fill the meter. The guest's personal drink plan is unchanged.

**Auto-host rounds · ฿1,500** finds matches automatically. It gives hosted-drinks-only
colleagues first choice in a tie, and has an on/off toggle in Service. If automatic
barfines are also on, eligible bookings run first. Someone must still serve every
drink. Hosted drinks spend the same finite entertainment budget as barfines;
a smaller round can leave a guest unable to afford a booking. Ordinary-trade
forecasts exclude these additional live rounds and temporary Buzz.

At a Hostess bar, the **Good Company** card in the room shows each host's availability.
Tap **Accept · ฿500** for an affordable request from a real customer. The fee goes
to the till once; the host leaves for **30 foreground seconds**, and her appeal
is removed until she returns. The card shows the countdown. Up to one/two/three
hosts can be away at once at venue stages one/two/three. Scroll for larger teams.
May and Fah prefer hosted drinks and never accept bookings.

**Service → Team** has the same controls, training and **Auto-accept for
฿3,000**. Once bought, toggle it on or off. It accepts eligible requests on live
ticks using the same consent, contract, rota, customer-budget and capacity rules.
It earns nothing on the startup menu or in offline estimates.

| Host level | Base appeal while present | Bar-fine fee | Training purchase |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| 1 | +0.6 | ฿500 | Starting level |
| 2 | +1.2 | ฿750 | ฿1,500; venue stage 2 |
| 3 | +1.8 | ฿1,000 | ฿3,000; venue stage 3 |

More appeal improves the venue's share of finite street customers, rather than
guaranteeing arrivals. Training stays with the person after expiry or rehire;
it does not change a fee already paid or shorten an active booking. Colleagues
who decline bookings can train their appeal without changing that preference.
Purchases retain a shift's operating reserve. Private activity stays off-screen.

New bookings retain their exact return time across reports and reloads. Older
saved bookings retain their original end-of-shift return, with no extra payment.

Automatic deliveries are a paid supplier agreement. They buy one complete delivery at your supplier price
when stock is below the delivery quantity and orders are waiting, retaining the next operating
charge. They cannot buy a partial pack or fill an idle bar speculatively. A manager needs stage 2, an on-duty bartender, deliveries and eight healthy
shifts, plus the hiring fee and operating reserve. Managers enable the offline
estimate. Check the current prices and requirements in **Service**.

Renovations require a local customer base and healthy shifts as well as cash.
The wealth of another property cannot satisfy those milestones. **Empire** shows
leases and their opening reserves. Finish the Blue Hour opening chapter before
buying a second venue. Hostess bars require 20 reputation; gogos require 45.

The combined active-play checks reach pool around 16–19 minutes, stage 2 around
34–40 minutes and a second property around 117–123 minutes. After stage 2, the
active policy improves pouring, trains staff and then saves for expansion.
First purchased leases are ฿12,000/24,000/60,000 for beer/hostess/gogo, plus
retained opening cash. Each earlier same-type purchase adds 25% of base; the
inherited Blue Hour does not count. Empire displays payment, retained float and
ongoing costs separately. Every fourteenth day has a visible 10% retiring-owner
offer, rotating hostess/beer/gogo. These are
simulation results for a particular investment strategy, not timers or promises
for every play style. [Economy details](ECONOMY.md) include the full measurements.

## If money runs out

Use **Close venue** to pause its payroll and overhead. Off-duty staff do not
charge wages. If an operating charge becomes unaffordable, the venue closes
before adding new idle debt, while keeping its staff records and upgrades. Contracts still count down.

At an empty till, a closed venue can take an **Owner-run recovery shift**. Anong
covers two minutes of overhead and the team is scheduled off. If the fridge is
empty, Bo provides six recovery drinks whose stock cost is taken only when a
customer buys one. Welcome and serve manually, then reopen with a sustainable
rota. Event closures must finish before recovery can begin.

## Away from the street

Enabled, viable managed venues can earn an estimated net amount for up to eight
real hours away. Only projected noon–1am windows count, at the game's clock
speed; late permissions do not extend offline hours. The calculation includes
limited demand, stock, saved dirt, each venue's peak windows and costs. An unviable projection pauses instead
of consuming your starting cash. It does not advance the visible clock, cleaning,
heat, raids, promotions, disasters or local milestones. **Journal** distinguishes
time away from eligible trading time and reports the estimate and limiting factors.
Owned stock is consumed; further whole deliveries must be funded by earnings from that
claim, never your opening cash. Manager income can fund that next pack;
leaving stock in the fridge lets drink service begin sooner.
Commission is included. Short claims retain fractional sales and costs, although
restarting a claim can reduce available pack working capital.

**Open managed shift** offers decisions across the same live two-minute shift.
The decision screens pause the model; continuing does not skip the clock or
create another stream of money. Exploring the district and Toybox sparring are
optional. The street continues after the festival.

## Toybox arcade controls

Open **Events** and select **Quick spar** or **Run the circuit**. The desktop
fight screen keeps each binding visible. Use A/D or Left/Right to move, W/Up to
jump, J to punch, K to kick and L for Burst when the meter is full.

- Hold **S** or **Down** to crouch and guard. A crouching fighter can duck a
  roundhouse.
- Hold crouch and press **J** for an uppercut, which can catch a jumping rival.
- Hold crouch and press **K** for a slower, longer-reaching roundhouse kick.
- On touchscreens, hold **Down** on the D-pad and tap **Punch** or **Kick**
  with your other thumb for the same moves.

## Follow the profit cycle

A visitor normally plans one or two rounds. The optional **Show visit plans**
control in Service tells you their actual plan and whether they are waiting,
drinking or finishing pool. A cyan glass is a drinking guest; yellow/red dots
still indicate waiting orders. They finish the final drink before freeing a seat.
Happy one-drink visitors can become regulars too.

**Your profit cycle** shows the last completed shift's actual customer profit,
manager income separately, its sample duration, and distinct arrival/order/sale/
completed-visit counts. Before the first report it uses the current shift.
This uses the cost of stock sold; the till's five-second cashflow also counts
whole stock deliveries, so the two figures can differ. Neither is a reward button.

- Turned-away guests: add seats or improve drinking turnover with music.
- Waiting orders lost: help serve or train a bartender.
- Empty room with spare service: improve frontage or invite customers.
- Stockouts: stock and deliveries come before advertising harder.

Each beer-bar stage unlocks more purchases: seating caps 3/5/7, frontage 1/3/5,
fridges 2/3/4, music 1/2/4 and personal bartender training 2/4/6. Stage-three
training adds 8-second service for ฿2,000 and 6-second service for ฿2,400.
The next locked upgrade shows its required stage. All still cost money; raising
a stage does not buy them for you. Check the
[price table](design/PROFIT_CYCLE.md#beer-bar-investment-tracks).

## Getting past the stage-two saving gap

Open **Service → Upgrades** for the **Regulars Club**. It costs ฿600, then
฿1,200 and ฿2,400, requiring 10/20/35 regulars at that venue. Keep a full pack's
cost and the next shift's operating costs in reserve. At each tier, 35/65/100%
of new arrivals join: they plan and budget for an extra round, capped at three. Visitors already in the bar are unchanged. Birthday and club still cap
at three drinks. The offer's estimated net change is not a guaranteed payout.

For an upgraded bar with no waiting orders, try the club and better music before

**15→12 seconds for ฿1,200**, then **12→10 for ฿1,600** per person. Training

shift report to see whether these purchases actually sell more drinks.

for 14 and 11 seconds. Drinking the final round uses the same timer before the

from four seconds to two. Current refills finish normally. Welcome still caps

## Saves and versions

There is one local campaign slot. New Game saves immediately; later saves happen
after decisions, every fifteen seconds, and on normal close or application pause,
with a backup retained. Waiting on the startup menu does not run the business,
write saves or claim offline estimates. Resume claims eligible time away once.
Current schema 15 retains sponsored orders, paid rounds, guest interests and Buzz.
Schema 14 also retains host training, auto-accept and fractional booking
return deadlines. Schema 13 visit plans/phases, beer-bar tiers and personal
training are preserved. Existing visitors finish their old saved visit rules; new arrivals use
the new plans. Older campaigns receive no free upgrades or refunds; existing
guests keep their budgets and visit limits. Save schema 9 retains staff cooldowns and actual service counters; older partial
work keeps its remaining wait. Schema 8 also retains celebration dates, reports and visitor patience.
The schema-7 fields preserve music, auto-serve, supplier/manager tiers, stock purchase
costs and the five-second net sample alongside existing timers and commission.
Existing manager/supplier appointments become level one without another fee. Migration does not
grant a promoter, compact layout or audience training for free. Migration from
pre-clock saves introduces noon on the campaign’s existing day,
with initially clean venues. Old campaigns preserve owned progress when migrated, so an existing stage-3 bar
will not revert to stage 1 for the new balance. Use a fresh campaign if you want
to play the revised opening. **New Game** and **Restart campaign** replace that
run and remove its old backup after confirmation; back up saves
first if you want to keep it. [Developer setup](../DEVELOPMENT.md) lists save paths.

The source branch and a published release may be different builds. Use the
checkout containing the changes you want to play, and avoid running two versions
against the same campaign at once.

The build version is in the lower-right corner. Include that corner when sharing
a screenshot for a bug report.

## Cold beer and bulk terms

Each better fridge tier adds forty stock spaces and one extra reputation point
per ten satisfied visits at that bar. With no upgrade that milestone earns +1;
with fridge levels one/two it earns +2/+3, up to 100 reputation. Buying the fridge
alone grants no reputation. Existing fridges benefit from future happy visits.

Buying a fridge upgrade at the Blue Hour reveals it in the room. The four tiers
add a shelf, a side compartment, twin doors and a cold drawer, with more frost
at each step. The bottles in the upgraded cabinet show roughly how full it is.

Supplier levels 0/1 buy six drinks for ฿228. Levels 2/3/4/5 buy twelve for
฿432/408/384/360. The button shows the current quantity and total bill. Eleven empty
spaces or ฿431 cannot buy a level-two delivery. Auto-stock also keeps cash for
the next operating charge; managed offline deliveries use their own earned funds.

## Closing clean-up and tomorrow's opening

After the last venue closes for the night, a free **15-second clean-up** starts
for all dirty venues, including those without a manager. A visible street button
counts down to noon. **Stay here** cancels the automatic jump for that night;
cleaning continues, and **Skip to open** stays on the bottom bar until noon.
Even an already-clean street gives you the full fifteen-second choice.

The countdown waits for late-trading venues to finish. Daytime closure and a
temporary event closure cannot end an otherwise scheduled late night. It advances
only during foreground street play, never on the start menu or through offline
estimates; its countdown pauses while the street is hidden by district/arcade
views. Normal clock rules still apply. Schema 10 preserves the remaining time
and cancellation across reloads; older saves start with no pending countdown.

At zero, completed cleaning is retained and the existing skip moves the clock
to noon. Stock is not bought, no income is awarded, and contracts age through the
skipped hours as before. Owner-closed or cash-shortage-closed venues stay closed.
The date already advanced at midnight, so the jump does not add another day.
