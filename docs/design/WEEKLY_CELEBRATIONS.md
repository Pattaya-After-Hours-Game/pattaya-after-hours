# Give day six a reason to become day seven

The player reports enjoying the opening but losing interest around day six.
The problem is a long gap between useful purchases and the next renovation.
This feature gives that stretch a visible, predictable destination and a short
change of pace. It does not add a login streak or a compulsory chore.

## Anticipation, preparation, payoff

- **Anticipation:** The street banner, Events and Journal announce each
  celebration one day ahead. Day six shows **Tomorrow: Anong's Birthday**,
  followed by the live event from noon on day seven.
- **Preparation:** No entry or event fee. Use the existing choices: stock, cold
  storage, seating, staff, music and serving upgrades. These are useful after
  the party too. A simple bar can participate without a specific hire or manager.
- **Payoff:** A full opening from noon to normal 1am close, with more potential
  visitors, quicker repeat orders and an optional third drink. Every sale still
  requires an actual customer, stock, budget and service.
- **Memory:** Bunting changes the room for the night. The actual trading report
  shows money earned, costs, happy visits, new regulars and lost business. The
  regulars remain because they were earned through good service.
- **Another reason to return:** Day fourteen is the Regulars' Reunion, day
  twenty-one Pim's Mixtape Party, day twenty-eight Bo's Thank-You Night, then
  those three rotate. They are neighbourhood gatherings, not weekly birthdays.

## Initial balance

The Blue Hour hosts; other venues share the extra street visitors but do not
copy the host's special drink limit or timing. Traffic is +40%, with combined
event traffic capped at 2.4 times the base for the current venue mix. The host's
attraction gains +2 before audience/peak/dirt modifiers. Paid promotions remain
optional; their fees, cooldowns and diminishing returns still apply.

Repeat timing is 35 → 26 seconds, or 25 → 19 with purchased music. Up to three
drinks replace the normal two, subject to the same spending budget. New party
visitors stay fifteen seconds longer and have ten extra seconds of patience.
The grace makes the rush more forgiving without making slow service irrelevant.
Already-running repeat timers are unchanged. Waiting orders keep their prices;
after 1am no new third drinks are requested. Purchased music remains useful.

Drink and pool prices do not rise; staff still draw wages and Nida still receives
her committed commission. Managers do not earn a party multiplier. The report's
trading contribution deducts consumed inventory, commission, operations,
promotion spending and fines incurred during the event; it excludes street-wide charges, management,
capital purchases and unused delivered stock. It is actual period trading, not
an assertion that all those visitors were caused by the celebration.

Unannounced random street events wait until after the celebration; previously
announced events continue. Closing the Blue Hour stops its traffic bonus. There
is no payout button, preparation fee or automatic reward for reaching a date.
Normal overnight skipping and real shift lengths remain intact.

## Respect the player's time

The date belongs to the simulated campaign. Offline estimates neither advance
nor use up celebrations and never apply their temporary bonus. Paused choices,
the start menu and practice cannot fast-forward the live campaign. Players can
put the game down; there is no real-world appointment or streak to maintain.
Save schema eight retains the active/last report, date and guest patience.
Older saves preserve their progress and receive the next calendar celebration,
without a retroactive cash grant or replay of a finished event.

## Verification and trade-offs

Thirty paired seeds for each of two businesses compare ordinary and celebration
openings: sixty pairs, 120 simulated trading days. A prepared stage-one bar uses
compact seating, music, a trained bartender and faster owner service; a basic
bar retains manual base service. These are controlled scenarios, not fresh-game
pacing runs or a guarantee of human results.

With ready-bartender cooldowns, prepared bars sold 51% more drinks and earned 57% more trading contribution in
aggregate. Basic bars sold 28% more and earned 27% more, with substantially more
lost customers. Preparation matters; advertising cannot substitute for service.
The same normal prices, costs, shared-demand limits and customer budgets apply.

Forty-two fresh campaigns still check the complete progression and alternative
strategies. Weekly rewards intentionally move late milestones earlier. The
stage-three acceptance floor allows 165 minutes versus the original approximate
180-minute target; early purchases, pool, stage-two and second-property bounds
remain unchanged. Measured results and limitations belong in
[ECONOMY.md](../ECONOMY.md#balance-verification) and [TESTING.md](../TESTING.md).
Device playtesting and whether players actually find week two more engaging
remain human questions; a simulation cannot prove retention.
